AAP and CDC schedules

Since 2025 the US has two childhood schedules that differ in some areas: the AAP schedule and the federal CDC schedule.

Where they differ

For example, the AAP recommends COVID-19 vaccination for all children aged 6 to 23 months and 2 HPV doses from 9 to 12 years. The CDC schedule leaves COVID-19 to a shared decision with your doctor and recommends 1 HPV dose at 11 to 12 years.

Which one this page uses

This page and VaxFile follow the AAP schedule. Ask your pediatrician which schedule their practice follows.

School and childcare

Each state decides which vaccines are required for childcare and school, and which exemptions are allowed. Check your state's health department website.

Not sure what your child has had? Ask your pediatrician. Many states also keep an immunization registry your pediatrician can check.

Moving to the US with children

  • Find a pediatrician early, ideally one in your health insurance network, and book a first visit.
  • Bring your child's vaccination records from abroad, with an English translation if needed.
  • Childcare and schools ask for an immunization record, often on a state form your pediatrician fills in. Start this before enrolment.
  • Your pediatrician compares your records with the US schedule and gives catch-up doses.
  • Common differences: the US gives hepatitis B at birth, hepatitis A, chickenpox, and a yearly flu vaccine from 6 months, but no MenB for babies.

Moving away from the US

  • Moving to the UK? See moving from the US to the UK.
  • Moving to Germany? See moving from the US to Germany.
  • Ask your pediatrician, or your state's immunization registry, for a printed immunization record before you leave.
  • Keep any school or childcare immunization forms. They list every vaccine with dates.
  • Your new country may schedule vaccines differently. For example, the UK, Germany and Spain vaccinate babies against MenB, and many European countries don't routinely give hepatitis A or a yearly flu vaccine.

Questions

Are vaccines required in the US?

There is no national vaccine requirement. Each state requires certain vaccines for childcare and school, and allows exemptions that differ by state.

Are childhood vaccines free in the US?

Most health insurance plans cover routine childhood vaccines. Children who are uninsured or on Medicaid may get free vaccines through the federal Vaccines for Children (VFC) program.

What if my child had vaccines in another country?

Take the records to your pediatrician. They check them against the US schedule, enter them in your child's record and give any missing doses.

Why are there two US schedules?

After changes to federal vaccine recommendations in 2025, the American Academy of Pediatrics published its own schedule. The AAP and CDC schedules now differ for some vaccines, such as COVID-19 and the number of HPV doses.
