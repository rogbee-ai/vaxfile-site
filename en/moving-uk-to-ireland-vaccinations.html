Moving from the UK to Ireland: your child's vaccinations

The UK and Irish schedules are close: both vaccinate babies against MenB and give a yearly nasal flu vaccine from age 2. The differences are in the timing, MenC, chickenpox, and the school-age vaccines. Here's what to check.

Last checked: 30 October 2026 · See also: UK vaccination schedule · Ireland vaccination schedule · All schedules

How vaccination works in Ireland

  • Baby and toddler vaccines are given by your GP, over five visits between 2 and 13 months. The HSE says all vaccines in the childhood programme are free from your GP.
  • The junior infants and first year vaccines are normally given in school by HSE school vaccination teams (by your GP in Sligo, Leitrim and Donegal). They are free, and the school sends a consent pack home.
  • The HSE asks you to bring your child's immunisation passport to each GP visit.

Key differences at a glance

What your child may need after the move

  • MenC. UK children born from July 2024 don't get a MenC dose, while Irish children do at 13 months. Ask your GP whether your child needs it.
  • Chickenpox. It depends on the date of birth in both countries. Bring the red book so the GP can see whether your child had MMRV.
  • School vaccines. If your child starts junior infants or first year in Ireland, the school vaccines are offered there. Your child may already have had some of them in the UK, so bring the record to the school team or your GP.
  • Different timings for the 6-in-1 and pneumococcal doses. Your GP compares the UK doses with the Irish schedule and decides whether anything is missing.

What happens to UK records

  • Bring the red book (Personal Child Health Record) and ask your UK GP practice for a printout of your child's vaccination history before you leave.
  • Register with a GP in Ireland and show them the UK record at the first visit.
