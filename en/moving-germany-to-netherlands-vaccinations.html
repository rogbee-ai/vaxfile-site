Moving from Germany to the Netherlands: your child's vaccinations

The Dutch programme covers most of the same diseases as the German one, but at different ages, without MenB or chickenpox, and with MenACWY for toddlers. Vaccinations are free and voluntary, and they happen at the consultatiebureau, not at the doctor's. Here's what to expect.

Last checked: 8 October 2026 · See also: Germany vaccination schedule · Netherlands vaccination schedule · All schedules

How vaccination works in the Netherlands

  • Register your address with your municipality. The youth health service (JGZ) then contacts you, and babies and toddlers are invited to the consultatiebureau (child health clinic).
  • From about 4 years, the GGD youth health service sends the invitations, for example for the vaccinations at 9 or 10 and 14.
  • All vaccinations in the National Immunisation Programme (Rijksvaccinatieprogramma, RVP) are free and voluntary. There is no measles requirement for daycare or school like the one in Germany.

Key differences at a glance

Germany (STIKO) Netherlands (RVP)
6-in-1 2, 4 and 11 months 3, 5 and 12 months (plus 6 to 9 weeks if the mother wasn't vaccinated against whooping cough in pregnancy)
Pneumococcal 2, 4 and 11 months 3, 5 and 12 months
Meningococcal B 2, 4 and 12 months Not in the programme
MMR 11 and 15 months 14 months and 3 years
Chickenpox 11 and 15 months Not in the programme
MenACWY Once at 12 to 14 years 14 months and 14 years
Pre-school booster Diphtheria, tetanus, whooping cough at 5 to 6 years Diphtheria, tetanus, whooping cough at 5 years
Teen booster Tetanus, diphtheria, whooping cough, polio at 9 to 16 Diphtheria, tetanus, polio at 14
HPV 2 doses at 9 to 14 2 doses at 10

Both countries offer an RSV antibody to babies in their first RSV season and rotavirus vaccine to young babies. Neither offers a yearly flu vaccine to healthy children.

What your child may need after the move

  • MenACWY as a toddler. Germany doesn't give it until 12 to 14 years; the Netherlands gives it at 14 months. The consultatiebureau checks whether your child should still get it.
  • Unfinished MenB or chickenpox series. These aren't in the Dutch programme. Doses your child already had still count. If a series isn't complete, ask the consultatiebureau what they advise.
  • Different timings for the 6-in-1, pneumococcal and MMR doses. The consultatiebureau compares the German doses with the Dutch programme and plans any missing ones.

What happens to the German Impfpass

  • Bring the Impfpass to the first appointment at the consultatiebureau or GGD. They compare it with the Dutch programme.
  • Vaccinations given in the Netherlands are recorded by RIVM. You can request an overview through MijnRIVM.
