Austria childhood vaccination schedule

The vaccines recommended in the Austrian vaccination plan (Impfplan Österreich 2026/2027) of the Federal Ministry of Social Affairs, Health, Care and Consumer Protection, for babies, children and teenagers, by age.

Last checked: 30 October 2026 · Source: Impfplan Österreich 2026/2027, version 1.1

Vaccines by age

This is the routine plan for healthy children. Most vaccines are part of the free childhood vaccination programme (kostenfreies Kinderimpfprogramm). A few are recommended for every child but aren't free; these are marked below.

RSV: babies born 1 October to 31 March get the antibody in their first week of life, before leaving hospital. Babies born 1 April to 30 September get it before their first RSV season. Rotavirus: Rotarix is 2 doses, finished by 24 weeks; RotaTeq is 3 doses, finished by 32 weeks. Flu: children vaccinated for the first time before their 9th birthday get 2 doses 4 weeks apart. HPV is free up to the 21st birthday.

Tick-borne encephalitis (TBE, FSME)

No Austrian state is free of TBE, so the plan recommends the vaccine for everyone living in Austria, from the 1st birthday. It isn't part of the free programme.

With FSME-Immun, the 2nd dose follows 1 to 3 months after the 1st, and the 3rd 5 to 12 months after the 2nd. The 1st booster is due 3 years later, then every 5 years. Ideally, doses are given before the tick season, in January or February.

Moving to Austria with children

  • Bring your child's vaccination records from abroad. Your paediatrician compares them with the Austrian plan and offers any missing doses.
  • Common differences: Austria recommends the TBE vaccine for everyone, can start MMR from 9 months, and recommends MenB, chickenpox and MenACWY for toddlers, which you pay for yourself.

Moving away from Austria

  • Ask your doctor for a complete copy of the vaccination record, or a printout of the electronic vaccination record (e-Impfpass), before you leave.
  • Your new country may schedule vaccines differently. For example, Germany requires proof of measles vaccination for Kita and school.

Questions

Which childhood vaccines are free in Austria?

The free childhood vaccination programme covers the RSV antibody, rotavirus, the 6-in-1 vaccine, pneumococcal, MMR, the two 4-in-1 boosters at school age, the hepatitis B booster, HPV up to the 21st birthday, MenACWY between 10 and 13, and the yearly flu vaccine. MenB, MenACWY for toddlers, chickenpox and TBE are recommended for every child but not free.

Why is the TBE vaccine recommended for everyone in Austria?

No Austrian state is free of tick-borne encephalitis, so the plan recommends the vaccine for everyone living in Austria, from the 1st birthday. It isn't part of the free programme. The first doses should be given before the tick season, ideally in January or February.

What if my child had vaccines in another country?

Take the records to your paediatrician. They check them against the Austrian plan and offer any missing doses. The plan says that missed recommended vaccines should in principle be caught up as soon as possible.
