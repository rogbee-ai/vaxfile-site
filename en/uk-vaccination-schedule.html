Which schedule applies to your child

The UK changed the childhood schedule in July 2025 and January 2026. The vaccines your child gets depend on their date of birth.

Born on or after 1 January 2025

The schedule above: MMRV at 1 year and 18 months, the 6-in-1 booster at 18 months, and the 4-in-1 pre-school booster at 3 years 4 months.

Born between 1 July 2024 and 31 December 2024

MMR at 1 year (given before MMRV started in January 2026). The 6-in-1 booster and MMRV at 18 months. MMRV again with the 4-in-1 pre-school booster at 3 years 4 months.

Born on or before 30 June 2024

Hib/MenC and MMR at 1 year, and no 18-month appointment. At 3 years 4 months: the 4-in-1 pre-school booster and MMRV, which also starts chickenpox protection.

If you're not sure what your child has had, ask your GP practice. They can check the record and book any missing doses.

Moving to the UK with children

  • Coming from the US? See moving from the US to the UK.
  • Register your child with a GP practice as soon as you have an address. Invitations for vaccinations come from there.
  • Bring your child's vaccination records from abroad, with a translation if they're not in English.
  • The practice compares them with the UK schedule and offers missing doses, following the UKHSA guidance for uncertain or incomplete vaccination status.
  • Common differences: many countries don't give MenB to babies, and chickenpox vaccination has only been routine in the UK since 2026. Your child may be offered doses they didn't get before.

Moving away from the UK

  • Moving to Spain? See moving from the UK to Spain.
  • Keep your child's red book (Personal Child Health Record).
  • Ask your GP practice for a printout of your child's vaccination history before you leave.
  • Your new country may schedule some vaccines at different ages, or offer vaccines the UK doesn't, such as hepatitis A or tick-borne encephalitis in some regions.

Questions

Are childhood vaccines compulsory in the UK?

No. Vaccination is not compulsory in the UK. The NHS offers the routine vaccines to every child and strongly recommends them, and you decide together with your GP or practice nurse.

Are childhood vaccines free in the UK?

Yes. The routine childhood vaccines are free through the NHS. Register your child with a GP practice to get invitations for each appointment.

What if my child had vaccines in another country?

Show your child's vaccination records from abroad to your GP practice. They compare them with the UK schedule and offer any missing doses, following the UKHSA guidance for children with uncertain or incomplete vaccination status.

When did the UK add the chickenpox vaccine?

From 1 January 2026, the UK replaced the MMR vaccine with MMRV, which also protects against chickenpox (varicella). Which doses your child gets depends on their date of birth.
