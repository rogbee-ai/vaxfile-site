Extra vaccines for some children

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children

The national schedule adds MenB at 2, 4 and 12 months and an extra pneumococcal dose at 6 months. In Western Australia, the Northern Territory, South Australia and Queensland, it also adds hepatitis A at 18 months and 4 years.

MenB in Queensland and South Australia

Both states offer MenB vaccines free through their own programs. See Queensland Health and SA Health for who is eligible.

Catch-up vaccines

Catch-up vaccines on the National Immunisation Program are free for everyone under 20.

Moving to Australia with children

  • Coming from the UK? See moving from the UK to Australia.
  • Get your child's overseas vaccines onto the Australian Immunisation Register (AIR). A GP or other vaccination provider can add them. Bring a record with vaccine names and dates.
  • Check the rules for your state before enrolling in childcare or kindergarten. Under No Jab No Play, Victoria, New South Wales, Queensland, South Australia and Western Australia require children to be up to date or on an approved catch-up schedule.
  • No Jab No Pay applies everywhere: your child must meet the immunisation requirements for Family Tax Benefit Part A supplements and the Child Care Subsidy.
  • Common differences: Australia gives hepatitis B at birth, MenACWY at 12 months and again in Year 10, and a free yearly flu vaccine to under-5s.

Moving away from Australia

  • Get a copy of your child's vaccination record from the AIR or your GP before you leave.
  • Your new country may schedule vaccines differently. For example, the UK gives MenB to babies and a yearly nasal flu vaccine from age 2, and Germany requires proof of measles vaccination for Kita and school.

Questions

Are vaccinations compulsory in Australia?

Not by law, but they are linked to childcare and payments. Under No Jab No Play, Victoria, New South Wales, Queensland, South Australia and Western Australia require children to be up to date, or on an approved catch-up schedule, to enrol in childcare or kindergarten. Under No Jab No Pay, children must meet the immunisation requirements for Family Tax Benefit Part A supplements and the Child Care Subsidy.

Are childhood vaccines free in Australia?

Vaccines on the National Immunisation Program are free. Catch-up vaccines on the program are free for everyone under 20, and the flu vaccine is free every year for children from 6 months to under 5.

What is the AIR?

The Australian Immunisation Register records every vaccine given in Australia. It's what counts for No Jab No Play and No Jab No Pay. A GP or other vaccination provider can add vaccines your child had overseas.

Is MenB part of the Australian schedule?

Not for all children. It's on the national schedule for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander babies, and Queensland and South Australia offer it free through their own programs.
