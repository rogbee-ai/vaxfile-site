Netherlands childhood vaccination schedule

The vaccinations in the Dutch National Immunisation Programme (Rijksvaccinatieprogramma, RVP) for babies, children and teenagers, by age. Run by RIVM and given by the youth health service (consultatiebureau and GGD).

RSV: babies born October to March get the antibody within two weeks of birth. Babies born April to September get it in September or October. Pregnant women are offered a whooping cough vaccine at 22 weeks, which protects the baby in the first months. The Dutch programme doesn't include chickenpox, MenB or a yearly flu vaccine for healthy children.

If your child was born earlier

The programme has changed in recent years. A few changes matter for older children:

RSV antibody (since September 2025)

For babies born from 1 April 2025, in their first RSV season. Older children don't need it.

Rotavirus (since 2024)

Only for babies, up to about 6 months. Older children who missed it don't catch up.

Second MMR dose

Now given at 3 years. Children born from 2016 to 2021 get it between 5 and 9 years, as part of the switch from the old age of 9.

Not sure what your child has had? Ask the consultatiebureau or, for school-age children, the GGD youth doctor. They check the record and plan missing doses.

Moving to the Netherlands with children

  • Register your address with your municipality. The youth health service (JGZ) then contacts you, and babies and toddlers are invited to the consultatiebureau.
  • Bring your child's vaccination records from abroad, ideally translated into Dutch or English.
  • The consultatiebureau or GGD compares them with the Dutch programme and offers missing doses.
  • Vaccinations in the programme are free and not compulsory.
  • Common differences: the Netherlands gives MenACWY at 14 months, doesn't vaccinate against chickenpox or MenB, and has no yearly flu vaccine for healthy children.

Moving away from the Netherlands

  • Request an overview of your child's vaccinations before you leave, through MijnRIVM or the request form on the RVP website.
  • Keep the vaccination booklet (vaccinatiebewijs) you received.
  • Your new country may schedule vaccines differently. For example, the UK and Germany vaccinate babies against MenB, and Germany and Spain vaccinate against chickenpox.

Questions

Are vaccinations compulsory in the Netherlands?

No. Vaccination in the Netherlands is voluntary. The National Immunisation Programme offers all vaccinations free of charge and invites every child.

Where do children get vaccinated in the Netherlands?

Babies and young children are vaccinated at the consultatiebureau (child health clinic). From about 4 years, the GGD youth health service sends invitations, for example for the vaccinations at 9 or 10 and 14.

What if my child had vaccines in another country?

Take the records to the consultatiebureau or GGD. They check them against the Dutch programme and offer any missing doses.

Why didn't my baby get the first 6-in-1 vaccine at 6 to 9 weeks?

That dose is only for babies whose mother wasn't vaccinated against whooping cough during pregnancy. If she was, the baby starts at 3 months.
